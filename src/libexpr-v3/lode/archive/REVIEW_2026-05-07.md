## Concerns

### 1. The 9 % implementation ratio is misleading-ly low — but real

44 functions declared in `ffi.hh`, only 5 implemented in `ffi.cc`.
Categories A–E (Parser, SymbolTable, Filesystem, Fetchers, Store)
have **zero** implementations. The ones that work are `EvalScope`
ctor/dtor + handle alloc/lookup/validate.

This is fine *as a milestone* (M1 of the migration plan was "sketch
the header pack"). It is **not fine** as a steady state. The risk is
that a beautiful type-level FFI sits next to v3 and never displaces
the actual call paths — the headers become decorative. Mitigation:
the next sprint must wire one full category end-to-end (filesystem
I/O is easiest; that's the proof-of-concept). (C — `ffi.cc` 238
lines, `ffi.hh` 532 lines.)

### 2. EvalState dependency is essentially unchanged

| Method | 3 weeks ago | Now | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `state.vm` | 110 | 110 | flat |
| `state.nixEvalState` | 52 | 55 | **+3** |
| `state.forceValue` | 10 | 6 | −4 |
| `state.symbols` | 6 | 1 | −5 (good) |
| `state.positions` | 8 | 9 | +1 |
| `state.baseEnv` | 12 | 12 | flat |
| `#include "nix/expr/eval.hh"` | 5 | 5 | flat |
| `treeWalkerToV3` / `v3ToTreeWalker` calls | n/a | 125 | — |

`state.symbols` shrinking 83 % is the only real win. Net shrinkage
is ~3–5 %, mostly in secondary accessors. The **load-bearing**
dependencies (`state.vm`, `state.nixEvalState`) haven't moved — and
`state.nixEvalState` actually grew. This is the steady-state risk
made concrete: declaring `EvaluatorSettings` doesn't help until
primops actually read from it. (C — grep against current
`src/libexpr-v3/`.)

### 3. `applyClosure` — the centerpiece of category G — is unimplemented

The signature is in `ffi.hh:407–408`. There is no implementation. The
current bridge path (`primV3CallBridge1` at `primops.cc:2929`) does
**not** go through `applyClosure`; it looks up handles in
`v3BridgeClosures()` directly and uses C++ exceptions across the
boundary.

So the FFI plan's G category — closure callback ABI, the inverted-
direction call that lets the store invoke v3 closures — is type-level
only. The fiber mechanism (A2) is wired in production but **for the
legacy bridge, not for `applyClosure`**. Until `applyClosure` is
implemented and at least one site routes through it, the contract
isn't tested in production. (C — `ffi.hh:407–408`,
`primops.cc:2929`.)

### 4. The `NIX_V3_SELF_DOT_MAX_LEVEL` opt-in hides an upvalue bug

#495's self-dot heuristic for `inherit (self.X) Y` in `lib.fix`
paths landed gated at level 0 (aaf02ae88, b9ce422b8, 02b1d28b1,
f8b6825ae). Why opt-in? Broader scopes expose an upvalue-capture
bug — wrong attrset captured for nixpkgs `isx86`. The team
correctly chose safety over optimism, but this is
**shape-of-the-bug C2 reappearing in a different guise**: rec / let
/ with identity is unstable across capture boundaries. The deferred
shapes work (PR9) is downstream of the same root cause.

### 5. The old bridge isn't retired

Per the closure-callback agent: "Old bridge path NOT retired;
fallback to tree-walker still active." `BRIDGE1_DEPTH=16` is a
band-aid; the real fix (closure callback through `applyClosure` on
a fiber) isn't wired. The bridge surface hasn't shrunk; it's been
*augmented* with new diagnostic and intrinsic paths.

## Was the trade-off right?

**Mostly yes**, with one nuance.

The team deferred PR2 (computed-goto, ~20 % perf), PR3 (latent
flags trap), PR9 (shapes, gated on C2), and PR10 (CLOSUREREC) in
favour of:

- Type-level FFI shape (#489/#490) — necessary precursor to
  everything
- `EvalScope` (#485) — A1 was the highest-priority adjustment
- `DerivationDescriptor` completion (#488) — A3
- Plugin ABI commitment (#492) — A9
- `lib.fix` intrinsic (#495) — addresses cardano-node #455 directly

This is defensible: the FFI shape and `lib.fix` intrinsic together
unlock the path to "v3 owns evaluation," which is the program goal.
The deferred items are perf polish that pays back forever after the
architecture stabilises.

**The nuance:** Computed-goto (PR2, ~1 day) and `CallFrame::flags`
(PR3, ~0.5 day) are still cheap. Skipping them once was right.
Skipping them twice in a row would be technical debt. They should
land in the next sprint regardless of what else does.

## Recommendation: next sprint priorities

1. **Implement `applyClosure`** (`Fallible<Value>` + `EvalScope` +
   fiber) and wire ONE production call site through it. This is the
   proof-of-concept that the FFI is real, not decorative. Suggested
   first site: `builtins.path { filter = ...; }` (the `addPath`
   filter callback at `primops.cc:2984`). *Half a week.*
2. **Implement category C (Filesystem I/O)** — 5 functions, all
   straightforward. End-to-end demonstration that the FFI shape
   *replaces* `EvalState` access, not augments it. *Two days.*
3. **Wire `EvaluatorSettings`** so primops stop reading
   `state.nixEvalState->settings` directly. Reduces the +3 growth in
   `state.nixEvalState` and unblocks pure-eval gating. *Two days.*
4. **Land PR2 and PR3** — computed-goto and `CallFrame` fix.
   *One day combined.*
5. **Root-cause C2 / the self-dot upvalue bug.** They're the same
   bug class. Until this is fixed, both shapes (PR9) and self-dot
   (#495 broader) stay gated. *One week.*
6. **Retire `primV3CallBridge1` fallback path** once `applyClosure`
   is wired and proven on cardano-node. Deletion + telemetry
   confirmation. *Three days.*

After this sprint: ~10–15 % of FFI implemented, real `EvalState`
shrinkage (50→30 in primops), one bridge path retired, two safety
floors fixed.

## Verdict

Direction is right. Velocity is high (783 commits / 3 weeks).
Architecture is consistent with the plan. But the work has piled up
at the *type level* without enough *call-site migration*; that
imbalance is the thing to watch over the next two sprints. If the
next milestone isn't **"first FFI category end-to-end with one
production caller migrated off `EvalState`,"** the FFI risks
becoming a parallel structure that nothing actually uses.

The C2 root-cause work is the secondary critical path: it gates
both shapes (PR9) and the broader-level self-dot heuristic (#495),
and it's the same bug class that's been recurring under different
names across the last three reviews. A directed root-cause sprint —
not another opt-in workaround — is overdue.
